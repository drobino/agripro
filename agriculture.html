From the soil to the table.

Four key areas shape how we support farmers — crop production, livestock, greenhouse farming and irrigation. Explore practical approaches for each.

01 OVERVIEW

Practical agriculture, built for real farms.

Ghana's farmers work across a wide range of conditions — from open-field crop farms to backyard livestock and controlled greenhouse environments. There is no single method that works everywhere, so we organize our resources around four core areas that cover most of what a farmer needs to know.

Each area below combines locally-relevant best practices with simple technology recommendations — nothing that requires expensive equipment or specialist training to get started.

02 • FOCUS AREAS

The four pillars.

01 / CROP PRODUCTION

Crop Farming

Good crop farming starts long before planting. We focus on soil preparation, seed selection and timing so that crops establish well and stay healthy through the growing season.

  • Test and improve soil health before planting
  • Choose crop varieties suited to local climate
  • Practice crop rotation to reduce pest build-up
  • Time planting around seasonal rainfall patterns
02 / ANIMAL PRODUCTION

Livestock

Whether it's poultry, goats or cattle, responsible livestock management protects animal health and farm income. We emphasize low-cost practices that smallholder farmers can realistically maintain.

  • Provide clean water and balanced feed daily
  • Keep housing well-ventilated and predator-safe
  • Follow a basic vaccination and deworming schedule
  • Separate sick animals to prevent disease spread
03 / CONTROLLED FARMING

Greenhouse Farming

Greenhouses let farmers manage temperature, humidity and pests more precisely, leading to more consistent yields — even simple shade-net structures can make a difference.

  • Start with low-cost shade nets or tunnels
  • Monitor temperature and humidity regularly
  • Use raised beds to improve drainage
  • Rotate crops to keep soil in the structure healthy
04 / WATER MANAGEMENT

Irrigation

Water is often the biggest constraint on yield. Efficient irrigation delivers water when and where crops need it most, reducing waste during dry spells.

  • Water early morning or evening to reduce loss
  • Consider drip irrigation for high-value crops
  • Mulch soil to retain moisture between waterings
  • Track rainfall to avoid over- or under-watering

03 • QUICK REFERENCE

Good agricultural practices.

A few habits that apply across almost every farming area we cover.

Know Your Soil

💧

🐛

📒
